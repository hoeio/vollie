# c3 memo-09: the gap after R72. Hexagon lane, more than one token per weight read, and what is left

Date: 2026-11-22. Author: Bonnie (`claude-fable-5-2`). **Ebony has not reviewed this yet**: this session had no
tool to start her, so the memo is written for her review (§8 lists what I ask her to attack). Not committed.
Nothing here is measured by me. Every price is *estimated* (arithmetic on R72 *device* inputs); quoted inputs carry
their own label. Script: [memo-09-work/bonnie_gap.py](memo-09-work/bonnie_gap.py) (self-check reproduces R72 §5's
6.90 / 6.21 / 6.22).

Subjects: Yi-1.5-6B, Llama-2-7B, DeepSeek-LLM-7B. Goal 12 tok/s printed on each = **79.2 ms per token**.

## 1. Verdict

- **No priced lever, and no priced stack, reaches 12 tok/s on any subject at its centre.** The best stack without a
  numerics change beyond the small ones is 11.7 / 10.6 / 10.6 tok/s (Yi / Llama-2 / DeepSeek), and it needs an
  owner decision (a trained sidecar head) plus a GPU kernel that does not exist yet.
- Without that owner decision the best stack is 9.9 / 8.8 / 9.0 at centres and 10.8 / 9.6 / 9.8 with a GPU kernel
  at the top of its band.
- **The Hexagon lane is the weakest of the three new levers**: about −7 to −9 ms per token at its centre, P about
  0.10, and it may not be reachable from the app at all. I price it below the GPU lane on every count.
- **One lever is new in kind and cheap to test: one weight pass for two token positions (lookahead, exact
  output).** R72 showed the stock body is bus-bound (ggml 25.8 to 26.7 GB/s against a 29.9 GB/s read), which is the
  only regime where a second position per pass is nearly free. It is worth −18 to −21 ms per token at its centre
  with a model-free guess, and it does not spend the +5 % budget.
- P(all three at 12): still about 0.005 (my judgement; was 0.001 in R72 §11, raised only because lookahead adds a
  lever that multiplies the others). P(Yi alone at 12): about 0.04.

## 3. The rule check on "more than one token per weight read"

- **Allowed as a method:** CAMPAIGN.md's metric row says "A method that drafts or commits several tokens per step
  counts committed tokens, keeps greedy output identical, and is scored on a fixed prompt set as well as the
  official prompt." So exact lookahead is inside the terms and does not touch the PPL budget.
- **Model-free guesses** (prompt lookup, n-gram, a successor table filled from the model's own earlier logits as in
  Token Recycling) are runtime. No file, no owner question.
- **Medusa-like trained heads: not covered by any existing decision; my reading is that they need the owner.**
  - For: the owner's 2026-11-19 decision allows a sidecar "generated by a committed script from the served file and
    a fixed calibration text", sha-keyed to the model; a guess head can never change the output, because every
    token is verified by the unchanged model, so it cannot spend the budget.
  - Against: that decision was about thresholds and orders. A head is trained weights (gradient descent on a GPU,
    about 5 hours on one A100 for a 7B model, *published*), 17 M to 150 M parameters, not computed on the phone
    (COMMON_RULES 1 says "computes once on the phone"). CLAUDE.md: "A different file or model is proposed to the
    owner first."
  - It is the lead's call to ask. Suggested wording for the owner, question (e): "May a sidecar that only proposes
    tokens, trained on a host from the frozen file and wikitext-2 train, and that cannot change any output token,
    count as a derived artefact?"
- **Closed history that this touches, and why it is different.** R20 (draft-model speculation) lost on the device
  and R69 Rule B scored KILL at r ÷ c_b = 1.17 (kill at ≥ 0.71, reopen at ≤ 0.61). Both stand as written. What is
  proposed here has no draft model and only one extra position. The R69 c_b was 3 cores at depressed clocks. With
  R72's A78 rate and the fourth core, r ÷ c is 0.86 for three A78s alone (*device* arithmetic) and 0.54 to 0.62 if
  the X1 delivers its R69 prediction (0.078 cycles per weight at 2,841 MHz, *estimated*, never measured on-core).
  That straddles the reopen line. A model-free device cell decides it (T2); I do not ask to re-score Rule B.

## 5. The three new levers in detail

### 5.1 Lookahead: two positions per weight pass (lever L, and M)

**Mechanism.** The pass that turns the last committed token into the next token's logits also carries one guessed
next token as a second position. If the guess equals the token the first position produces, the second position's
logits give the token after it: two committed tokens for one read of the weights. If not, the second position's KV
entry is dropped. The head runs for the second position only after the guess is confirmed, so the head cost stays
per committed token.

**Why now.** A second position costs compute, not bytes. R72 found the body bus-bound at stock, so compute is the
resource with slack.
- Compute cost of batch 2 (issue count, *estimated*): the Q4_0 unpack is shared and only the two `sdot`s are
  repeated, so about 1.45 × a gemv per weight (1.9 × for batch 3).
- Compute time for Yi at batch 2: 1.45 × 3.11 GB ÷ c. With c = 55 GB/s (3 A78 at 2,419 MHz plus the X1 at its
  prediction) that is 82 ms, under the 104 to 121 ms bus time, so kB ≈ 1.0. At R72's L4 clock caps (0.64 to 0.74 of
  full) it is 110 to 128 ms, so kB 1.0 to 1.2. **The clock cap during a sustained decode decides this lever.**
- Caution: ggml's own hot cell at 4 threads read 19.07 GB/s (L5, a 1 MB matrix, CPU ÷ wall 0.917), below its cold
  rate. That cell is dominated by call overhead, so the hot ceiling through ggml is not measured.
- ggml as built may not do this: as I recall its Q4_0 repack path, batch sizes under four go through the gemv once
  per position, which reads the weights twice (*recalled*; Tom to check, also for the KleidiAI path the app runs).
  If so it needs a batch-2 kernel (small: the 4x4 gemv with two activation vectors).
- Batch 3 (a chain, 1 + p + p² tokens at kB(3) 1.3) prices worse than batch 2 for p below about 0.55 (Yi's body;
  hand count), so it matters only for a trained head.

**What p is.** One guess, one position, no draft model:

| source | value | label |
|---|---|---|
| prompt lookup on the official prompt, campaign 1 R16 | 1.20 to 1.27 tokens per evaluation | *device* / *host* |
| Prompt Lookup Decoding, Llama-3.1-8B / Qwen3-8B | τ 1.30 / 1.24 (draft length not capped at one) | *published*, ESP Table 1 |
| Lookahead Decoding at 30 to 60 parallel positions | τ 1.38 to 1.51 | *published*, same table |
| ESP (mask-token probing, ICML 2026) at 30 to 60 positions | τ 1.56 to 1.71 | *published*; each mask is an extra position, so useless at batch 2 |
| Token Recycling, 80-node tree, Vicuna-7B | τ 2.70; one-node value not published | *published* |
| Medusa-1, frozen backbone, Vicuna-7B | 2.2 × with a tree; first-head top-1 not in the text I read (I recall about 0.6: *recalled*) | *published* / *recalled* |

So p for one model-free guess is 0.25 (0.15 to 0.40), and the trained head's 0.60 is a recollection to be checked
on the host before anyone leans on it. The break-even is p ≈ kB − 1.

**What kills it:** kB > 1.2 on the phone at decode clocks, or p < 0.20 on the official prompt or the prompt set.

**Heat.** The pass does about 1.45 × the arithmetic in the same wall time. If the phone answers with a lower cap,
kB rises. Only a real decode shows that; the model-free cell is an upper bound on the lever.

### 5.2 GPU lane (lever G), updated

- Upstream `docs/backend/OPENCL.md` (read 2026-11-22) verifies Adreno 750, 810, 830, 840, X1-85, X2-90. On our
  class it says: "A6x GPUs with a recent driver and compiler are supported; they are usually found in IoT
  platforms", and that A6x phones are likely unsupported because of outdated drivers. Supported types include Q4_0
  and Q6_K.
- Our phone reports a driver dated `05/11/25` and compiler E031.38.01.08 (*device*). That is newer than the "old
  phone driver" case the document describes, which moves P(the kernels build) up a little; it stays unproven.
- No Adreno 6xx decode number was found again. PowerInfer-2 on a Snapdragon 8+ Gen 1 printed 6.5 tok/s on Llama-7B
  (*published*, second-hand through a survey), which is our K0, on a newer chip, with its own sparsity.
- g band 8 (4 to 15) GB/s. I am above Ebony's 5 (3 to 10) because the hardware limit is far away: the probe's
  1.88 GB/s is about 7 GFLOP/s on a GPU of the order of 1 TFLOP/s (*recalled*), so the kernel is the limit, and
  the upstream kernel is a tuned one. Disagreement recorded; her number is the conservative one.
- The lane's batch-2 cost falls on its compute, which is its bound, so in a stack with L the lane is priced at
  g ÷ 1.45.

### 5.3 Hexagon 780 lane (lever H)

**Is it reachable from an app on this phone?** Not established. P about 0.5.
- The route would be FastRPC to the cDSP in an unsigned process domain, with our own DSP-side library.
  - *Published* (Qualcomm forum, Hexagon SDK 4.4.0.1 on a Snapdragon 888+): the SDK's calculator example failed as a
    signed PD and **ran as an unsigned PD**. That is a development device, not a retail Samsung.
  - *Published* (Check Point, 2021): a regular Android app has no permission to run its own code on the DSP except
    through unsigned PD on the chips that allow it; Google blocks the FastRPC drivers for third-party apps on Pixels
    by SELinux. I found nothing either way for Samsung.
  - Android 12 and later: the app must declare `libcdsprpc.so` with `<uses-native-library>`; it loads only if the
    vendor lists it as a public library. Not checked on this phone.
- QNN / Genie is closed for a second reason beyond the converted file: Qualcomm's LLM support list (QAIRT 2.33
  notes) starts at SM8550 and does not include SM8350; block-wise 4-bit weights need v69 and weight sharing v73
  (*published*, ExecuTorch PR 15965).

**Read rate and Q4 support.**
- llama.cpp's Hexagon backend runs Q4_0 `MUL_MAT`. v68 support was merged on 2025-11-24 (PR #17394), HVX only, no
  HMX. The author's numbers on a v68 chip (8cx Gen 3), Llama-3.2-1B Q4_0: 7.21 tok/s on the NPU against 26.92 on the
  CPU; "Runs models, but really slow". The maintainer: v68 lacks the int32 → fp32 conversion instructions and "the
  performance is not going to be that good". Early runs crashed the cDSP unless one HVX thread was used.
- From 7.21 tok/s on a 0.70 to 0.77 GB body: **g about 5 to 5.5 GB/s** for the whole graph (*estimated* from
  *published*). I take g = 5 (3 to 8) for a gemv-only lane. The DSP's own read rate is not published anywhere I
  found.
- The current upstream Snapdragon document lists libraries for v73 to v81 only, and a session of about 3.5 GB of
  address space. A lane that takes 15 to 20 % of the body (0.5 to 0.75 GB) fits.

**Costs that the GPU lane does not have.**
- Weights must sit in FastRPC shared (dmabuf) memory, which is pinned and not file-backed: 0.5 to 0.75 GB more
  unevictable memory. For Llama-2 and DeepSeek, which need a file-backed body because anon42 was not held, that is
  the wrong direction.
- Dispatch: the layers are serial, so the lane can only take a matrix that runs beside another (`up` beside `gate`,
  `v` beside `q`/`k`): about 64 dispatches per token. At 0.1 ms each (0.05 to 0.25, a guess) that is 6.4 ms, about
  half of the lane's gain.
- The bus: CPU + GPU already sum to 46.5 GB/s of a nominal 51.2. A third master adds little to the read sum, so H
  is an alternative to G, not an addition (+0.6 ms in the last stack row).
- Building it needs the Hexagon SDK and toolchain on the host.

**Price:** −6.8 / −9.0 / −7.9 ms at the centre; it can be a loss. **I would not build it before G has failed for a
reason H does not share** (for example the OpenCL compiler), and not before the zero-cost reachability check (T4).

## 6. What I searched and did not find (2026-11-22)

- **Activation sparsity on SiLU models, 2025 to 2026:** WINA (ICLR 2026) and WiSparse (2026-02) are the newest;
  both are already in memo-04 and memo-06. Nothing new that is training-free and holds 50 % inside +5 % on our PPL
  protocol. CATS, R-Sparse and the output-side methods stay closed (memo-01, memo-05).
- **Other runtimes on this chip class:** no MNN, mllm or PowerInfer-2 number for a Snapdragon 888 and a dense 7B.
  mllm and PowerInfer-2's NPU work is QNN on v73 and later (memo-03).
- **KV and head:** nothing beyond C7 and KV Q8_0 (memo-08: KV is under 3 % of a token's bytes).
- Two searches returned summaries only (unsigned PD by chip generation; Samsung's SELinux policy). The Hexagon
  reachability statements above are therefore weak, which is why T4 exists.
